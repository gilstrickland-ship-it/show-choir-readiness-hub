# Show Choir Public-Signal Discovery

## Conclusion

This file reflects a deeper public-source pass completed on March 1, 2026, with added emphasis on 2025-2026 sources.

The public-signal pass supports one clear first problem:

**High school show choir students struggle to stay rehearsal- and competition-ready because the instructions, practice materials, and expected next steps are scattered across informal channels, which leaves them confused, behind, and stressed.**

This is the strongest validated problem from the current evidence set because it is:

- student-facing,
- operational rather than purely emotional,
- repeated across both direct student posts and supporting public web sources,
- and solvable by a focused app without requiring school-wide adoption on day one.

## What Was Actually Found

### Strongest direct student signal

On February 14, 2026, a student posted in `r/Showchoir` before their first competition saying they were new, did not know how competitions worked, and did not want to ask their choir because they would sound "stupid."

Source:
[First comp tomorrow, I'm scared](https://www.reddit.com/r/Showchoir/comments/1r4c9dj/first_comp_tomorrow_im_scared/)

Why it matters:

- It is recent.
- It is clearly student-authored.
- It shows both information confusion and social friction.
- It points to a high-value problem: students need safe, low-friction access to "what happens next" information.

### Strongest practice-support signal

In a November 14, 2025 `r/MusicEd` thread, a show choir director described needing to provide students with part tracks and choreography videos because rehearsal time is not enough for students to master material.

Source:
[Show choir is killing me](https://www.reddit.com/r/MusicEd/comments/1owhaz0/show_choir_is_killing_me/)

Why it matters:

- It describes a recurring structural gap, not a one-off complaint.
- It identifies the current workaround already in use.
- It strongly suggests students depend on fragmented support materials outside rehearsal.

### Strongest public web support

The January 17, 2025 student newspaper article below explicitly describes students needing to keep thinking about what changed, watching rehearsal videos "pretty religiously," and managing a season that consumes large parts of their schedule.

Source:
[Dancing into Show Choir Season](https://www.thelittlehawk.com/68483/news/dancing-into-show-choir-season/)

Why it matters:

- It is student-centered.
- It confirms outside-of-rehearsal catch-up behavior.
- It shows the problem is recurring during season, not isolated to one poster.

### Added recent corroboration from 2025 school coverage

Two additional 2025 school sources reinforce that this is a repeated operational issue, not a one-off anecdote:

- On January 24, 2025, a student newspaper article from Maryville described show choir season as starting in the summer, with rehearsals as early as 6:00 a.m. during the week and 10-hour Saturdays.
- On January 30, 2025, a Carrollton schools article described students spending months preparing multiple performance numbers before annual competition.

Sources:
[Show choir season in full swing](https://www.themaryvilleforum.com/articles/featured/show-choir-season-in-full-swing/)

[Carroll students participate in annual show choir competition](https://www.carrolltoncityschools.net/article/2001479)

Why they matter:

- They confirm the prep burden is sustained and seasonal.
- They strengthen the case that students need a clearer way to track readiness over time.
- They support the catch-up/accountability hypothesis without relying on old sources.

### Best evidence that current solutions are fragmented

The Northwest Rankin audition page distributes prep across multiple assets: sheet music, a full performance recording, isolated voice part tracks, a handbook, an ensemble guide, and a choreography tutorial.

Source:
[High School Show Choir Auditions (Copy)](https://www.nwrchoralmusic.com/high-school-show-choir-auditions--1)

Why it matters:

- It shows the standard workaround is "many separate files and links."
- It validates that students need a lot of structured prep material.
- It supports the hypothesis that the pain is not lack of content, but lack of a simple, unified readiness workflow.

An even stronger current artifact is the Muscatine River City Showcase handouts hub, which sends users to separate MP3 tracks, part tracks, Google Classroom, choreography videos, dance review videos, and event handbooks.

Source:
[River City Showcase Handouts](https://sites.google.com/muscatine.k12.ia.us/rivercityshowcase/handouts)

Why it matters:

- It is a real operating model for show choir support materials.
- It shows the "solution" today is a bundle of disconnected destinations.
- It sharpens the product wedge: unify the readiness workflow, not just store files.

### Supporting evidence that competitions are operationally complex

The Jackson Prep 2026 competition page dedicates a large explainer section to awards, finals, solo events, parking, dining, and logistics.

Source:
[Show Choir Masters 2026](https://www.jacksonprep.net/arts/showchoirmasters)

Why it matters:

- Competitions are operationally dense enough to require dedicated orientation.
- First-timers likely need a simpler student-facing explanation than host pages provide.

## Rejected Or Secondary Problems

### Social belonging / clique stress

This surfaced in public posts, but it is weaker as a first app target because:

- it is emotionally real but broad,
- it is hard to solve with a narrow, utility-style MVP,
- and the evidence did not point to a clear product-shaped intervention.

### Attendance / commitment

This also surfaced, but it trends toward program policy and director enforcement rather than a student-first app. It may matter later as a secondary feature (visibility, reminders, accountability), but it is not the sharpest first wedge.

## Source Gaps

### Indexed X / Facebook

Public search did not produce usable student pain posts from X or Facebook. Results were either:

- generic account pages,
- login-gated Facebook pages,
- or non-student promotional content.

This means those platforms should be treated as low-confidence supplementary channels for this niche unless you use manual, logged-in research later.

### Google Trends

A direct Google Trends result set was not accessible through the available web tooling in this environment, so trend validation is not included in this first pass.

That does not invalidate the problem choice, but it does mean seasonal search-demand confirmation remains a follow-up item rather than completed evidence.

## Confidence Shift After The Larger Pass

The deeper pass increased confidence in the same core problem rather than changing it.

What changed:

- The recommendation now depends less on older 2022-2023 Reddit posts.
- There is stronger 2025 support from student newspapers and school pages.
- There is better concrete evidence that the current workflow is fragmented across tracks, videos, portals, and handbooks.

What did not change:

- X and Facebook still did not produce reliable public evidence.
- Google Trends is still unverified here.
- The best first app remains a student readiness utility, not a social network or admin suite.

## Final Recommendation

Use this as the product-definition handoff:

**We are building for high school show choir students who struggle to stay rehearsal- and competition-ready because the instructions, practice materials, and expected next steps are scattered across informal channels, which leaves them confused, behind, and stressed.**

## Immediate Build Direction

The most defensible product direction from this evidence is a narrow student utility that helps with readiness, not a broad social app.

Good first product shapes:

- a personal readiness hub,
- a rehearsal change digest,
- a "what to practice next" checklist,
- a competition explainer for first-timers,
- or a catch-up view for missed rehearsals.

Bad first product shapes:

- a general social network for show choir kids,
- a director administration suite,
- or a broad all-in-one platform before validating retention.
